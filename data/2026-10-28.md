카테고리: bioRxiv

  <summary>상세 내용 (Details)</summary>

- **연구 배경 및 동기 (Motivation)**: 항체 설계 분야에서 높은 단백질 폴딩 신뢰도가 실제 항체-항원 결합으로 이어진다는 가정하에 구조 예측 모델이 제로샷(zero-shot) 디지털 스크리닝 도구로 널리 사용되고 있으나, 기존의 검증은 소규모 테스트 세트, 제한된 표적 다양성, 훈련 데이터 누수 위험 등으로 인해 한계가 존재했습니다. 따라서 대규모의 독립적이고 편향되지 않은 데이터셋을 통해 구조 예측 모델의 실제 결합 분별력과 스크리닝 신뢰성을 엄밀하게 평가할 필요성이 있었습니다.

**원문 초록 (Abstract)**: Structure prediction models are increasingly deployed as zero-shot digital screens in antibody design, operating under the assumption that high folding confidence implies true binding. However, rigorous validation of this premise has been limited by small test sets, narrow target diversity, and the risk of data leakage from training-adjacent structures. Here we introduce POISK: Patent-extracted Organization of Immunoglobulin Sequence Knowledge, a large-scale dataset derived from the US patent literature, comprising 89074 antibody-antigen records extracted from 10110 patents using an LLM-based pipeline. From this resource, we curate a balanced benchmark set of 22494 complexes: 11247 patent-validated binders without a known resolved structure paired with putative non-binders constructed from sequence-similar human repertoire antibodies. Using this dataset, we benchmark six leading structure prediction models (AlphaFold-Multimer, Boltz-2, IntelliFold v2, OpenFold3, Protenix-v1, and Protenix-v2) on their ability to discriminate binders from decoys. Epitope localization remains unreliable: the best model contacts annotated epitope residues in only 62.8% of cases, indicating that binding discrimination does not establish accurate epitope localization. Our results suggest that current folding models provide useful but limited signal for antibody screening and should be complemented by orthogonal methods. We release POISK as a community resource for future benchmarking and model training.
